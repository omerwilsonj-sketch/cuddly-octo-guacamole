# Beyond the Plateau: Common Mistakes Intermediate Spanish Learners Make

Reaching the intermediate level in Spanish is a huge achievement. You can hold a conversation, order at a restaurant, and follow the news. But many learners get stuck here—the infamous "intermediate plateau." Often, it’s not for lack of trying, but because of a few common mistakes that hold them back from true fluency.

## 1. Relying on "Neutral" Spanish
Many learners stick to a very safe, textbook version of Spanish. While safe, it sounds robotic. To reach the next level, you need to start incorporating regional slang, natural fillers (like "pues," "vaya," or "mira"), and local expressions. Embracing a dialect is how you start sounding human.

## 2. Neglecting the Subjunctive
Intermediate learners often find workarounds to avoid using the subjunctive mood because it’s "too hard." However, the subjunctive is essential for expressing desires, doubts, and emotions. Avoiding it limits your ability to express complex thoughts and makes your Spanish sound simplified.

## 3. The Fear of Sounding "Wrong"
At the beginner level, we expect to make mistakes. At the intermediate level, we feel like we *should* know better, so we stop taking risks. This fear of making a grammatical error leads to speaking less, which is the fastest way to stall your progress. Remember: fluency is about communication, not perfection.

## 4. Translating Literally from English
Intermediate learners often think in English and translate word-for-word. This leads to "Anglicisms"—sentences that are grammatically correct but sound completely unnatural to a native ear. To fix this, you need to learn "chunks" of language and idiomatic expressions that don't have a direct translation.

## 5. Lack of Targeted Immersion
Watching movies is great, but at the intermediate level, you need more focus. If you want to work in Mexico, stop watching shows from Spain. If you want to live in Argentina, listen to Argentine podcasts. Your ears and tongue need to adapt to the specific rhythm and melody of the region you care about.
