One word. Two countries. Big trouble.

If you studied Castilian Spanish, "coger" just means "to take" — "voy a coger
el autobús" is completely normal in Madrid. Fly to Mexico City and that same
word is vulgar, and people will notice right away. Swap it for "agarrar" and
you're fine. This is exactly why learning Spanish by dialect matters when
you're moving abroad instead of just passing a class. Learn the Mexican Spanish
and Castilian Spanish you'll actually hear before you travel.

CAPTION:
3 mistakes that scream "tourist" in Spanish.

"Estoy embarazada" does NOT mean you're embarrassed — it means you're pregnant.
"Soy caliente" isn't "I'm hot," it's a much more awkward thing to announce; say

These are the Spanish mistakes that give learners away in a single sentence. If
you're learning how to speak Spanish for travel or a move abroad, these false
friends are the ones worth memorizing first — small fixes, instant local energy.

3 Spanish words English can't translate.

Why "vosotros" breaks everyone's Spanish.

If you learned Spanish in a US classroom, you probably never heard "vosotros" —
then you land in Madrid and it's everywhere. Spain says "vosotros habláis."
Mexico, and almost everywhere else, says "ustedes hablan." Latin America mostly

learning how to speak Spanish for a move to Spain versus Latin America. Pick
your dialect before you pick your verbs.

VIDEO 6 — reel_you_dialect.mp4
TITLE: One word for "you"? Spain, Mexico & Argentina disagree
CAPTION:
One word for "you"? Spain, Mexico & Argentina disagree.

Say "you" wrong in Buenos Aires and you're a tourist before you finish your
greeting. In Argentina, "tú" barely exists — it's "vos sos" and "¿tenés
tiempo?" In Mexico it's standard "tú," with "ustedes" for everyone. In Spain
it's "tú" for one person and "vosotros" for "you all." Move country and the
whole rule changes. If you're learning Spanish to relocate or build a career
across borders, this is the Rioplatense, Mexican, and Castilian Spanish
distinction that matters.

VIDEO 12 — reel12_chilean_speed.mp4

CAPTION:
Why Chilean Spanish has a reputation for being impossible to follow.

Chilean Spanish drops the S and swallows the ends of words, which is why it
sounds so fast. "Sí poh" is how "sí pues" comes out. "Voy al tiro" means "I'm

the S, swallow the ending, and you're halfway to sounding Chilean. If Chilean
Spanish ever felt impossible to follow, that's why — and it's very learnable
once someone shows you the pattern.
